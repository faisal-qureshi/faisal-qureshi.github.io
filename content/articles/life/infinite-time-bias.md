We all know someone who wishes they had told their late partner that they loved them. We all have this regret or will have it, that we wish we had spent a little more time with our parents.

If only we had known we had so little time with them.

## Infinite Time Bias

We walk through life, with this delusion that we have our whole life ahead of us. We squander away precious time in the most wasteful activities, or inactivities, assuming the universe holds a bottomless reservoir of tomorrows. 
#### ==🟡AND SO WE END UP WASTING TIME, ALL THE TIME.

The truth is that, nature does have infinity, but it is not for us. It is for time itself. We all have a very finite set of moments to live.

## Now Imagine

If every living thing, humans, animals, plants, everything had a digital number floating above, down to milliseconds floating above every head. The digits quickly clicked down, showing us exactly how much time this being has left before it "switches off". This concept was best portrayed in the [[in-time-2011 | 2011 Science Fiction Movie "In Time"]].

## Scary Thought?
One would think that watching a loved one's digits hit zero would be agonizing. That such apparent certainty of our mortality would freeze us in terror. And maybe it will.

However...

## I Think

It would ignite a fierce, desperate urgency. The urgency to leave nothing unsaid, leave no incomplete tasks. We would be ready when the time comes.

At the same time it would give us comfort, for we would know that we have some time - even if finite and limited. Especially for those who live with the conscious awareness of death.

## The Bitter Truth

When anything becomes a tangible, vanishing resource, it suddenly becomes valuable for us. So, if we were to be awakened from the delusion of time abundance, the paralysis of procrastination would also disappear. You would abandon passive waiting and act immediately, because the cost of hesitation would be glaringly obvious.

But the most chilling reality isn't the hypothetical timer. It is the biological truth we ignore.

You already have an expiration.

It remains unseen. Every moment you spend delaying, doubting, or waiting for the stars to align, your hidden clock advances. The scarcity is real. The limit is active.

Stop treating your life like an endless draft. The flawless moment is a myth designed to keep you stagnant. Look at the people you cherish, look at the passions you’ve buried, and accept the truth: your unseen expiration is already in motion. Do not let it run out while you hesitate to begin.
